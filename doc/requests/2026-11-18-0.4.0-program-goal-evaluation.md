**Target commit:** `0.4.0v - Add goal-based AI investment evaluation`

## 2. Staff workflow and layout

Extend the existing investment-comparison workspace with a compact goal selector and **Manage goals** action. Use existing controls, dialogs, icon components, and responsive styling.

1. Staff selects a saved goal or creates one.
2. Structured constraints apply deterministically, with their effects visible. AI fit is initially **Not evaluated** unless a matching cached result exists.
3. Staff clicks **Evaluate goal** once to assess the relevant comparison options. No paid calls on selection, typing, saving, sorting, filtering, map movement, or page load.
4. The table gains a **Goal fit** column with the goal's short name as a sublabel. Each assessment has a clickable info hint with a concise reason, supporting evidence, and material gaps.
5. Staff can sort/filter by fit and inspect excluded or unknown options.
6. The existing **Explain** action optionally explains the overall comparison using this active goal and the current evaluation/filter state. Evaluation does not automatically trigger a second explanation call.
7. **Clear goal** restores the previous ordinary comparison ordering/filter state, removes goal-only restrictions and the fit column/status bar, and preserves the staff's budget, category, radius, horizon, and other non-goal inputs.

One active goal at a time. Multiple saved goals are supported, but no combining goals or custom weight editor in this release. Selecting a goal must not silently widen the existing comparison scope, change equipment category, or change the budget. A goal allowing several types restricts the current universe; it does not require building cross-category investment optimization. Show a clear conflict/empty state when the current category is outside the allowed set.

## 5. Separate hard requirements from AI fit

Maintain separate fields in the evaluation/view model:

1. Existing financial/feasibility eligibility, including missing information.
2. Goal requirement status: **Meets / Does not meet / Unknown**, with deterministic reason codes.
3. AI fit: **Strong / Partial / Weak / Insufficient evidence**, or **Not evaluated** before assessment.

Allowed equipment IDs and goal dates are enforceable structured requirements. Budget and site feasibility retain their existing rules. For combinations, every constituent type must be allowed. AI cannot override any of these results.

Natural-language goals can contain restrictions the app cannot enforce structurally. AI may identify a conflict or missing evidence, but must label it as a qualitative assessment, not silently turn arbitrary prose into executable filters or database queries. If description contradicts structured fields, return a concise goal-level warning and request staff clarification; do not silently choose one interpretation or produce a confident ranking.

A vague objective such as “make the most ethical choice,” with no measurable priority, should return **Needs clarification** with a short actionable question, not a fabricated moral score. An unsupported goal such as “serve the most teenagers” should return insufficient evidence where relevant data is absent.

### Fit rubric

- **Strong:** supplied evidence supports the principal qualitative objective with no material unresolved gap about that objective.
- **Partial:** evidence supports some priorities but shows a material tradeoff or incomplete support.
- **Weak:** supplied evidence shows poor alignment with the stated qualitative objective.
- **Insufficient evidence:** a key fact needed to judge that objective is missing or too ambiguous. Missing evidence is not negative evidence.

Use these labels, **not 0–100 scores or percentages**. No probability of success or invented precision. Do not produce an opaque composite score combining financial efficiency, morality, and fit. An option can fit qualitatively yet be unaffordable or fail a date requirement; make the distinction visible.

## 6. Filtering, ordering, and compact status bar

Provide **All options**, **Strong or partial fit**, and **Strong fit only** controls plus a clear way to show excluded/unknown rows. Default to a transparent all-options view when a goal is first selected. Never silently make incomplete records disappear.

Fit-filter views require both a qualifying AI fit and no known failure/unknown in applicable hard requirements or existing eligibility checks. Unknown options remain available in the all-options/needs-information view, but cannot be presented as fully suitable. Avoid guessing suitability from a missing budget or cost value; preserve existing financial semantics.

When goal sorting is selected, group comparable, requirement-meeting options first and sort Strong → Partial → Weak → Insufficient evidence/Not evaluated; keep unknown/failed eligibility groups clearly separate. Within equal fit groups retain the prior deterministic comparison order and stable ID tie-breaker. AI does not reorder numerical facts or generate the final table order. Do not give a “best investment” badge merely because a row has strong fit.

Add a small status bar above the table, for example:

> Low disruption · 4 match · 2 excluded: equipment/date · 2 need information · 4 shown

Counts are code-generated over the **full current comparison result set before goal filtering**, not only visible rows or the current page. Use mutually exclusive buckets and provide a short explanation of their meaning:

- **Excluded:** a definite hard requirement or existing eligibility failure, regardless of AI fit.
- **Need information:** no definite failure, but an applicable requirement is unknown or AI cannot assess the objective.
- **Not evaluated:** otherwise assessable rows awaiting a current AI evaluation.
- **Match:** requirements met, existing eligibility satisfied, and Strong/Partial fit.
- **Low fit:** requirements met and Weak fit.

Buckets must sum to the comparison total; omit zero-count labels for compactness. Show **Shown / Hidden** separately according to the selected filter. Reason subtotals may overlap when an option fails multiple rules; label that fact in the detail rather than pretending they sum to excluded count. Clicking counts/reasons reveals the appropriate existing rows or a compact detail panel, without AI calls.

Header info hint: “AI assessment of the selected goal using available evidence. Costs and requirements are checked separately.” Each row hint should be about 1–3 short sentences, with evidence labels and missing facts accessible on click. Do not show raw field names or JSON. Use current icons, accessible button names, keyboard/touch behavior, focus handling, and non-color-only statuses. Important unknowns/errors remain visible without opening a tooltip.

## 7. AI input and assessment contract

Assemble an authoritative server-side snapshot of the active goal revision and existing comparison options. Send only necessary facts: goal text/dates/catalog restrictions, current scenario and budget assumptions, candidate names/types/descriptions/site notes, feasibility, supplied schedule estimates, cost components and sources, existing deterministic coverage/cost metrics, and applicable evidence gaps.

Do not send resident identities, private request pins, account information, or unredacted resident notes. Aggregated request statistics may be used only if already calculated reliably. The previously reported synthetic demand points have no request dates or notes: do not invent them. Accessibility cost is not accessibility certification; public access is not wheelchair access; equipment name is not evidence of user age; location is not evidence of income or demographic identity.

Represent field provenance/uncertainty sufficiently to distinguish synthetic assumptions, staff-entered notes, and verified facts. Preserve synthetic labels throughout UI and exports. Never turn model knowledge into claimed site facts or fetch external sources as part of this feature.

For each assessable option, require structured output containing its exact option ID, fit enum, short reason, references to supplied evidence identifiers, material missing information, and any qualitative conflict. Include an optional goal-level clarification/warning. Do not ask for a second long narrative during evaluation.

Validate output schema, unique IDs, full expected coverage of the bounded option set, evidence references, enums, and text lengths. Reject unknown or duplicate IDs, unsupported references, and malformed output. Do not silently assign Weak to omitted rows. On failure, retain deterministic constraints and show evaluation unavailable; do not substitute invented AI results. Schema validation cannot prove the truth of every sentence, so tightly constrain prose and facts rendered by the UI.

Evaluate existing combination options as whole options using their union coverage and constituent evidence, not averaged single-site fit labels. Never count overlapping requesters twice. Where constituent conflicts matter, mention them briefly. Do not invent alternatives or alter the candidate set.

Use one bounded request for the current comparison where practical. Default maximum **20 options** per evaluation. If scope exceeds the configured limit, ask staff to narrow the comparison before making a paid call; do not silently evaluate the first page or launch uncontrolled batches. Goals consisting only of structured restrictions can show deterministic filtering without a gratuitous AI call; explain that no qualitative assessment is needed.
