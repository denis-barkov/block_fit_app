## 2. Public discovery and distance guidance

Use the existing selected equipment category and reference-location workflow. A manually placed reference pin or an existing explicitly chosen current/saved location is the origin. Never silently overwrite a resident's saved request location when they explore another origin. Do not request browser geolocation automatically.

Compute straight-line geographic distances through the existing shared geometry utilities. Sort matching sites by ascending distance, with a stable ID tie-breaker. Do not sort only the visible markers or current result page and call that the nearest across Brooklyn and Queens: perform the selection over the relevant accessible inventory, with efficient existing filtering/pagination.

Show these default configurable bands:

| Exact distance range | Public label |
| --- | --- |
| 0 through 0.25 mile | Nearby |
| More than 0.25 through 0.5 mile | A little farther |
| More than 0.5 through 1 mile | Farther away |
| More than 1 mile | Consider transportation |

Store thresholds once in consistent internal units, deriving display conversions centrally. Use unrounded values for classification. Label distance **“Approx. distance”**, with a compact shared explanation: **“Straight-line distance; the actual journey may be longer.”** Do not describe these as walking distances, walking times, or an individual's ability to walk. Small distances can cross inaccessible barriers.

Without an origin, retain normal browsing and show the existing reference-location action; do not invent distances. Preserve existing equipment verification, condition, public-access, and synthetic-data labels. Closed, unusable, private, or otherwise ineligible records must not become travel recommendations. Unknown records retain their existing uncertainty labels and cannot be promoted to verified destinations.

If there is no eligible matching site within **0.5 mile**, display **“Nothing close by? Explore travel options.”** This is deterministic and free of AI calls. At exactly 0.5 mile the trigger does not activate. Also offer a modest manual “Explore travel options” action when nearby sites exist. If no matching eligible equipment exists anywhere in current coverage, show the normal empty state and request-equipment action, not an AI call with invented destinations.

## 3. Two-step helper in the existing interface

### Step A — Help me choose

- Open a compact panel/sheet in the existing search/map interface, not a separate application or chat page.
- For anonymous users, display a brief preview and **“Sign in or create an account to explore suggestions.”** Preserve category and reference pin across authentication without placing exact coordinates in login URLs. Do not automatically generate or charge after login; retain an explicit generation action.
- For residents, one click requests a shortlist of **up to three** matching sites. This is a comparison among a bounded candidate pool, not a claim to evaluate every journey in both boroughs.
- Cards show site name, available relevant equipment, approximate distance, one short reason to consider it, and any material uncertainty. Keep AI suggestions separate from the distance-sorted public results.
- Permit a farther site to be suggested when comparable evidence supports a useful alternative, such as mapped rail landmarks near both endpoints. Explain that evidence without calling the site faster, connected, or easier to reach as an established fact.
- Provide “Explore this location,” external directions, and copy actions. Do not hide known public facts behind AI generation.

### Step B — Explore this location

- A resident can select a shortlisted site or invoke the same helper on a specific eligible site directly.
- Present a focused, short suggestion using the same evidence contract. Selecting a card itself must not trigger an unexpected paid call. Reveal a cached/prepared explanation immediately; otherwise offer an explicit “Generate suggestion” button.
- Prefer reusing useful explanation data from Step A over paying for a second equivalent response. If a separate focused generation is requested, apply the same limits and communicate any remaining cooldown.
- A valid example, only when supported by both landmark observations: “A rail station is mapped near this location and another near your starting point. Transit may be worth checking. Open Maps to compare the actual journey.”
- Nearby stops at both ends **do not prove connectivity**. Do not state “take the train from A to B,” “you can get there by bus,” or similar instructions based only on proximity.
- Show a concise shared notice: **“Suggestions use mapped landmarks, not verified routes or live service. Confirm your journey in Maps.”** Do not repeat a large disclaimer on every card.

UI states: missing origin, no candidates, sign-in required, collecting map evidence, generating, cached result, incomplete evidence, cooldown/quota reached, provider failure, and changed search. Keep public results and external directions working in every AI failure state. On origin/category/inventory change, mark previous suggestions stale or clear them; never attach them to the new search.

## 4. Evidence collection from the current map

First inspect and document usable source IDs, source-layer names, feature classes, station/stop name fields, feature identifiers, supported zoom ranges, and attribution requirements in the current installed style. Do not infer a station from an arbitrary point or from a rail line. Preserve required map attribution.

Create a small map-evidence adapter, separate from UI components and prompts. Prefer source-feature access where appropriate; rendered labels alone may be suppressed by styling or collision rules. Even source-feature queries only cover loaded tiles and may return duplicates or features outside the visible viewport.

### Bounded collection, not a citywide transit dataset

- Build a deterministic pool of up to **six nearest eligible matching sites**, plus a directly selected site when using the focused mode. Never discard a directly selected site merely because it lies outside the shortlist pool.
- Collect evidence around the origin and those destinations within a configurable **800-meter landmark radius**. This is a demo search radius, not a guaranteed walkable catchment or accessibility standard.
- Validate feature classes and coordinates, filter by geographic distance, deduplicate stable IDs or a documented conservative fallback, and cap retained landmarks at **three per mode per endpoint**.
- Use a consistent validated detail zoom and explicit loading status. Verify that each endpoint's search extent is covered at that detail before treating its observations as comparable. A source-wide loaded event alone does not establish endpoint coverage.
- Do not silently rank a site worse because its tiles were never loaded. Track `observed`, `partial`, `unavailable`, or equivalent evidence status. An empty result means “No landmarks found in the inspected map data,” never “There is no transit here.”
- Do not sweep Brooklyn/Queens, load thousands of tiles, add a map per result card, or visibly pan/zoom the resident's map to scrape evidence. Use a bounded map-source loading approach only if supported by the current stack, provider terms, and reasonable resource use. Clean up temporary resources and cancel superseded work.
- If complete comparable sampling cannot be achieved simply, use existing available observations as clearly incomplete context and **do not use uneven landmark availability to favor destinations**. Keep deterministic distance/equipment comparisons and external directions as the fallback. This fallback is authorized; adding a transit service is not.
- Rail geometry may provide background context but cannot establish stops, usable service, bridges/pedestrian access, or a connection between endpoints. Never infer routability from touching lines or matching station names.

Do not make AI wait indefinitely for tiles. Use a configured collection timeout and a bounded payload. Reuse a context snapshot for repeat requests; normal panning/zooming must not create new paid calls or arbitrarily reorder an already generated shortlist.

### Evidence trust

Map features obtained in a browser and sent to the server are untrusted client input. Server-side validation must allowlist types, enforce size/name limits, check coordinates and proximity, and recompute distances. Re-fetch equipment eligibility and public facts from the authoritative database; never trust browser-supplied equipment or cost facts. Label map observations as map-derived, not independently verified by government. Do not store them as authoritative inventory or planning inputs. A client cannot assign its own trusted status, role, quota, or cache identity.

## 5. AI responsibilities, prompts, and output

Use separate `.txt` files under the existing `prompts/` convention for shortlist and focused suggestion instructions, sharing the existing safe placeholder renderer. Keep named placeholders validated, literal, and single-pass. Stable instructions must not be duplicated in handlers. Use the already configured OpenAI model; do not increase model cost or enable browsing/tools for this feature.

Send a compact structured snapshot: selected category, eligible candidate IDs/names, computed distances, known equipment/condition facts, bounded landmark observations and their confidence/loading states, plus explicit limitations. AI may choose among supplied candidate IDs and explain qualitative tradeoffs. Code owns distances, eligibility, source validation, and external URLs.

Do not send account IDs, email, private saved addresses, or exact origin coordinates to OpenAI. The model normally needs only aggregate proximity facts and relevant public landmark names. Do not infer barriers from its memory. Names and other map text are data, not instructions; sanitize length and keep them out of trusted instruction messages.

Keep ordinary shortlist explanations to about **35 words per option**, focused suggestions to about **90 words**. Define output bounds in configuration. Use the existing structured-output mechanism with candidate IDs and constrained reason/evidence codes where useful. The server validates IDs, duplicates, eligibility, lengths, and cited evidence references. Exact displayed facts and links should be rendered deterministically. Reject unsupported structured claims rather than showing them. Schema checks do not establish the truth of arbitrary free-form prose; keep prompts and prose scope narrow.

If evidence is weak, say so briefly and compare known location/equipment facts. Do not manufacture a transport benefit just to demonstrate AI. Do not output a claim that a synthetic demo equipment location exists in real life; retain the demo label and do not encourage travel to invented equipment. Real transit landmarks around a synthetic site do not make that site real.
