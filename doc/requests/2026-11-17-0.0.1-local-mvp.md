## 1. Purpose and completion standard

Build a useful public-investment planning prototype: help City staff compare where a defined outdoor fitness investment could address more recorded unmet demand within an available upfront budget, while exposing ongoing costs and feasibility uncertainties. Public equipment discovery is the supporting resident benefit.

This supplements existing City planning, engagement, budget-request, and maintenance processes. Staff can consult public references and carry an exported brief into their existing review. Do not imply agency integration, official acceptance of requests, verified savings, or authority to approve installations. Do not recreate 311 or a capital-project management system.

Implement one connected application, not three isolated mockups. A resident request must persist and affect the staff comparison. Staff inventory edits must affect public listings and coverage. Deliver a coherent local demonstration with automated regression coverage; do not leave fake-success buttons, placeholder persistence, or invented AI output presented as live.

## 7. Calculation specification

Put deterministic calculation functions in a tested domain module, reused by server endpoints. Server results are authoritative. Do not call an AI model for arithmetic, ranking counts, geocoding, or feasibility decisions.

For one selected category, radius R, and data revision:

1. Select distinct active requesters within the pilot dataset. Ignore withdrawn requests.
2. Existing coverage requires same-category, non-archived equipment that is public, verified, usable, and confirmed publicly accessible. Unknown records remain browseable but do not assert coverage. State that unknown inventory may make unmet demand appear larger.
3. A requester is already covered if geodesic distance to any eligible existing record is less than or equal to R.
4. For a candidate, count uncovered requesters within R. For a combination, count the union of those requester IDs, never sum individual counts.
5. Use one radius for baseline and candidate comparison. Default R = 200 m; configurable bounded staff scenario controls. Default horizon = 10 years. Public search radius may differ but cannot change the planning calculation silently.

Costs per candidate:

```text
upfront = equipment + installation + site preparation + accessibility
replacement events = positive multiples of replacement interval strictly before horizon end
lifecycle = upfront + annual maintenance × horizon + replacement cost × event count
cost per newly covered requester = lifecycle / unique newly covered count
```

No replacement at time zero or exactly at the horizon end. “No replacement planned” is explicit; an unknown interval/cost is not assumed zero. Preserve missing-value flags. If an amount required for a metric is unknown, that metric is incomplete/N/A. Upfront budget fit may still be known when only ongoing costs are missing.

Compute low/base/high costs consistently over the same horizon; these are scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals. Combined costs are the sum for distinct selected installations, with no invented economies of scale. Show upfront fit separately from lifecycle cost, which is not the same budget constraint.

To highlight a straightforward comparison: among non-excluded, fully costed, suitable-for-comparison options with positive new coverage and base upfront cost within budget, highlight the lowest base lifecycle cost per newly covered requester. Label it “Lowest modeled cost per newly covered requester,” not “Best public investment.” Show ties and flag when high costs exceed budget. Unreviewed options remain visible as provisional but cannot receive that highlight. Do not rank unknown or zero-coverage options as free or best.

Export a downloadable Markdown brief with category, radius/approximation, horizon, data revision/date, inventory assumptions, unique coverage, cost breakdown/ranges, feasibility, missing inputs, sources, optional public project link, and interpretation limitations. Include any AI explanation only when current and labeled. No private origins, resident notes, identities, or credentials in export. Sanitize user-entered content and allow only safe external URL schemes.
