## 2. Separate navigation and route layouts

Implement distinct public/resident and staff route layouts while reusing authentication, accessible components, design tokens, and business logic. Do not create a second application, database, or independent authentication system.

Use `/staff/login` and `/staff/*` for the staff experience. Preserve existing public/resident URLs where practical; safely redirect old staff URLs if they already exist. Scope old URL redirects to avoid loops or granting access.

### Access and navigation contract

| Visitor/session | Public/resident routes | Staff routes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signed out | Public navigation, sign-in/register; private resident actions require login | Redirect protected pages to `/staff/login`; no staff data |
| Resident | Public navigation plus own requests/account/logout; no staff menus | Deny staff access, with a clear route back to the public app |
| Staff | Public browsing only; do not display resident My requests/account actions | Dedicated staff shell, staff navigation, staff session/logout controls |

- Staff navigation should include only implemented staff functions, such as investment comparisons and equipment/candidate management. Do not add empty sections to make the panel appear larger.
- Use a near-black staff navigation background, implemented as a token in `design/`. Keep shared typography and component styling. Also label it “City workspace” so the distinction does not rely on color alone.
- Staff shell contains a clearly labeled “View public site” link. Leaving the workspace does not require logout. On the public site, a staff session may see “Return to City workspace” and logout, but not resident-only actions.
- Staff identity controls must never link to a resident profile screen. Include a staff settings link only if there is an actual relevant screen; otherwise show identity and logout.
- Resident navigation must never show staff options. A quiet public/footer “City staff sign in” link is acceptable as an entry point; do not confuse it with resident registration.
- Login pages use the corresponding shell and wording. Staff login has no registration link that could imply public staff creation.
- Staff credentials entered at ordinary login redirect to the staff workspace. Resident credentials entered at staff login do not enter it; show a clear role mismatch and a resident-login link. Do not silently switch accounts or discard an existing session. Provide explicit logout/switch-account control if needed.
- Preserve intended resident destinations/category selection after resident login. Accept return URLs only from an allowlisted same-origin route set appropriate to the authenticated role; prevent open redirects.
- Logout ends the session and returns to the relevant sign-in/public entry point without leaving protected data visible in client state. Back-button navigation and expired sessions must revalidate access.
- Enforce all permissions on server APIs and loaders, not merely in menu rendering. Loading a staff URL, changing a role field, or calling an API directly cannot bypass authorization.

Maintain responsive layouts and keyboard behavior. Avoid rendering the wrong navigation briefly while session state is loading; use a neutral loading state.

## 3. Make the investment workflow understandable

Inspect any current labels or controls that suggest “investment approval,” “approved access,” or automatic selection. There is **no approval workflow** in this MVP. Correct misleading terminology and explain what the existing calculation actually does; do not invent an approval feature.

Make the progression clear without building a large wizard:

1. **Choose equipment and assumptions:** selected category, coverage radius, upfront budget, planning horizon.
2. **Inspect unmet demand:** distinguish recorded requests already covered by existing equipment from requests lacking coverage.
3. **Select candidate sites:** click a candidate on the map or its table row and select up to the existing supported combination limit.
4. **Compare investment options:** individual candidates and the selected combination show unique new coverage, upfront cost/budget fit, lifecycle cost, cost per newly covered requester, and uncertainty.
5. **Export for review:** download evidence for a human planning discussion; no funding request or approval is sent.

### Required clarity

- Use a consistent map legend and distinguish existing equipment, demand origins, and candidate sites by labels/shape as well as color. Candidate IDs/names must match the table and details panel.
- Show selected category and radius prominently. Label distances as straight-line approximations, not walking-time estimates.
- Explain “newly covered” near the metric: active requesters of the selected category without eligible existing coverage who fall within the candidate radius. Combination coverage counts each requester once.
- Preserve feasibility statuses `unreviewed`, `suitable-for-comparison`, and `excluded`. Explain “suitable for comparison” as an analytical screening status, not permission to build, ownership confirmation, or budget approval.
- If a result is highlighted, state its exact basis: “Lowest modeled cost per newly covered requester” among eligible, fully costed, positive-coverage options within the base upfront budget. Preserve existing eligibility rules and explain ties or no eligible result.
- Make missing costs, zero coverage, excluded sites, and over-budget options understandable. Unknown is not zero; zero coverage yields N/A. Show upfront budget fit separately from lifecycle cost.
- A combination must show unique coverage and overlap without implying summed individual demand is the benefit. Do not change economics merely to make demo results look attractive.
- Preserve staff-triggered AI explanations as optional interpretation of calculated results. No live calls for routine testing or data generation. No AI-generated cost estimates.
- Use brief helper text and an optional compact “How this comparison works” disclosure. Avoid long instructional blocks on the main screen. Keep export wording aligned with the screen.

## 4. Broader, more plausible synthetic demonstration data

Replace the conspicuously diagonal/regular layout with a reproducible, irregular dataset. Keep the three equipment categories; “more equipment” means more locations/records, not scope expansion into new activity types.

Suggested defaults, centralized in seed configuration:

- One expanded, contiguous NYC pilot area, large enough for several separate demand clusters.
- Approximately **60–90 synthetic demand origins**, **12–18 existing equipment records** across multiple sites, and **6–8 candidate sites** across the three categories.
- At least two viable comparison candidates for pull-up bars, with nontrivial overlap; some candidates for other categories. Give staff enough alternatives without requiring citywide optimization.

Use a seeded pseudo-random generator or curated irregular coordinates, not new random data on each startup. Spread origins across several plausible land/street blocks: dense clusters, smaller clusters, and scattered outliers. Avoid obvious repeated diagonal/row patterns, identical offsets, water, and implausible placements in the middle of major roads. A small curated set of land areas is sufficient; do not add a GIS pipeline or routing service.

Include visible scenarios:

| Scenario | What it demonstrates |
| --- | --- |
| Demand near usable existing equipment | Already served requests do not inflate new benefits |
| Dense uncovered cluster | Strong candidate coverage |
| Two candidates with partial overlap | Unique combined coverage and duplicate avoidance |
| Scattered underserved requests | No single candidate meets every need |
| Candidate with little/no matching demand | Location alone does not imply investment value |
| Expensive or uncertain candidate | Budget and missing inputs change interpretation |
| Unverified or unusable equipment | A map pin is not proof of usable coverage |
| Standalone equipment | A park association is optional |

Keep coordinates/category/status/cost relationships internally consistent. Include different verification/access states and feasibility states. Unknown ownership or access must not be presented as safe public availability.

All invented equipment, requests, candidates, and costs must be clearly labeled synthetic/illustrative in records, UI, and export. Real neighborhood geography is not evidence that fictional equipment exists there. Do not label invented equipment as genuinely field-verified; any simulated verification is explicitly part of the demo fixture.

### Seed safety and isolation

- Separate fixture identity from human-created records. Use stable seed IDs/version metadata so an explicit demo-seed update can replace old fixture records without deleting real submissions, manual inventory, or staff accounts.
- Keep seed updates idempotent. Do not reseed on ordinary startup or every deployment. Provide a preview/count summary before destructive replacement of an old demo dataset.
- Implement fixture migration/update through the maintained Makefile/script workflow. Do not require an operator to delete rows manually.
- Retain small deterministic calculation fixtures for unit tests; do not replace their known-answer cases with random data. Add assertions for the new dataset's intended scenario relationships rather than fragile exact rendering snapshots.
- Automated-test data must not pollute presentation demand. Use server-controlled test-account/fixture classification, isolated test comparisons, and cleanup. Public users cannot assign themselves a special data classification. Clearly separate synthetic presentation data from automated smoke-test mutations.
