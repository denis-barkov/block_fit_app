# BlockFit NYC

BlockFit NYC helps City staff compare outdoor fitness investments using resident requests, existing equipment coverage, and transparent cost assumptions. Residents can find equipment, check what is known about access, and request what is missing near a private location. Staff can maintain the inventory, model candidate sites under a budget, and export a comparison brief.

The 0.5.1 local beta uses a demonstration dataset in and around Forest Hills, Queens with 153 synthetic request origins, 29 illustrative equipment records, and 15 candidate sites. Maps are framed around all five boroughs. New resident and staff pins must fall within Brooklyn or Queens, including Coney Island and the Rockaways. Demo verification and cost assumptions are simulated. BlockFit NYC is an NYCEDC project concept. A recorded request can inform planning discussion, and City approval and funding follow existing processes.

## Run locally

Use Node.js **24.22.3** (see `.node-version`), npm 11, and Make. The local app requires no Cloudflare credentials or OpenAI key.

```sh
make setup
make dev ENV=local
```

Open the loopback URL printed by Wrangler, normally `http://127.0.0.1:8787`. If that port is occupied, choose a free one in `.env.local` by changing `APP_ORIGIN` before restarting. Use the exact configured URL for session cookies and origin checks.

Setup applies the SQL migrations and creates the local dataset and two demonstration accounts. Open **`.blockfit/demo-accounts.json`** in a private local editor to retrieve the generated logins. The file is ignored by Git. Use **Sign in / Register** for the resident login or self-registration, or open **City staff sign in** at `/staff/login` with the staff demo credentials. The dedicated staff shell starts at `/staff`. **View public site** and **Return to City workspace** preserve the staff session. Select your initials beside **Sign out** to view account details. Residents can also change their password there. Keep synthetic coordinates in any demonstration. The default local database lives in `.blockfit/local-d1/` and survives restart.

## Product behavior

- **Public discovery.** Browse without login and filter by equipment type or site name. Set a reference pin to sort matching equipment by approximate straight-line distance. The distance bands are Nearby (through 0.25 mi), A little farther (through 0.5 mi), Farther away (through 1 mi), and Consider transportation (beyond 1 mi). The actual journey may be longer. Site details, copying the destination, and Apple Maps and Google Maps links are public. Maps receives the destination only. Demo pins open a map view. Sites flagged as NYC Parks-maintained link to [NYC311 park maintenance](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01060).
- **Resident requests.** Register with a display name, unverified email login, and a passphrase of at least 15 characters. Save one private pin in Brooklyn or Queens, even where there are no equipment listings. Choose whether to use it for new requests. Existing pins outside the service area require a move before new requests can use them. Turning that preference off leaves active requests in place. Moving the pin requires confirmation and moves all active requests together. Withdraw active requests before removing the saved pin. Removal archives the pin with a date and keeps past requests in your history. A database constraint allows one active request per account and equipment type. A suggested installation site is separate from the request location.
- **City workspace.** Staff can add, edit, and archive parks, equipment, and candidate sites. Choose equipment and assumptions, inspect already covered and unmet demand, then select candidates from the map or table. Compare individuals and combinations of up to three candidates. The table shows unique new coverage and how many requesters fall in overlapping radii. The status above the table shows how many options are displayed. Run the comparison again after relevant changes. Briefs carry the comparison revision and exclude private origins, notes and identities.
- **Program goals.** In **Investment comparisons**, use **Manage goals** to save a short name and description plus optional earliest start, target completion, and allowed catalog types. Candidate editors can enter estimated start and ready dates as planning assumptions. Required dates with missing estimates are unknown. Every site in a combination must meet the selected requirements. Selecting a goal preserves the comparison inputs. Use **Selected sites only** next to **Compare sites** to narrow a larger comparison. Select all available sites and uncheck the ones you want to omit. A combined option is calculated when two or three sites are selected.
- **Equipment types and proposals.** Staff can create, rename, deactivate, or reactivate catalog entries. A rename follows the same ID through inventory, requests, and new comparisons. Residents can select **Suggest another type** in a new request and track it in **Request history**. Approval records demand at the submitted location and date, or links to an existing active request. Residents can have five pending proposals and submit ten per UTC day, including submissions later withdrawn or rejected. New types use normal map and planning defaults. Candidate costs stay unknown until entered. Inactive types are unavailable for new selections, travel suggestions, planning, and reconfirmation. Staff can still inspect linked records, and residents can still withdraw requests.
- **Planning model.** The default is a 200 m straight-line radius and a 10-year horizon. Baseline coverage requires listed, verified, usable equipment with confirmed public access. The calculation counts overlapping requesters once. It shows upfront budget fit, undiscounted lifecycle cost, cost per newly covered requester, and low/base/high assumptions. Unknown costs stay unknown, and zero new coverage yields N/A for cost per requester. The highlight considers fully costed, suitable options with positive coverage within the base upfront budget. Suitability is an analytical screening status. Participation counts do not establish population-wide need, unique people, or installation feasibility.

Use [NYC Parks Properties](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Recreation/Parks-Properties/enfh-gkve) for park references and the [Capital Projects Portal](https://capitalprojects.nycplanningdigital.com/about) for public project context. The demo has no imported Parks inventory. Use the optional public project link in candidate records to carry a reference into the brief.

## Technology and configuration

- TypeScript, React, Vite and Tailwind provide a responsive interface. MapLibre GL JS and OpenFreeMap supply the interactive basemap with attribution. Turf provides distance and radius geometry. Lucide provides action and info icons. Select a circled info icon to show contextual help. Lists and coordinate fields remain available when the map fails. [Lucide license](public/licenses/lucide-react.txt)
- Hono and Zod serve same-origin APIs in a Cloudflare Worker with Static Assets. In production, Cloudflare Pages forwards the public origin to the private Worker through a service binding. D1 stores users, sessions, inventory, demand, and AI request state. Better Auth handles credentials and sessions. Resident ownership and staff privileges are enforced in the Worker. D1 enforces login and registration throttles and AI cooldowns.
- Edit `appIdentity.name` in [`config/app.ts`](config/app.ts) to rename the app across the browser, fallback page, and exported briefs. The same file owns the description, monogram, tagline, icon paths, project credit, and brief filename. Edit the original [BF artwork](design/brand-monogram.svg) or [design tokens](design/tokens.css), then use `make brand-assets` with librsvg to regenerate the SVG and raster assets and the link preview. Rebuild and restart to see changes. Cloudflare resource names use the existing deployment configuration.
- `config/map-area.ts` defines the map camera bounds and uses the same vendored [NYC Planning shoreline geometry](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/City-Government/Borough-Boundaries/gthc-hcne/data) for the outline and pin validation. The Brooklyn and Queens features were retrieved November 18, 2026 through the [NYC Planning ArcGIS layer](https://services5.arcgis.com/GfwWNkhOj9bNBqoJ/ArcGIS/rest/services/v_NYC_Borough_Boundary/FeatureServer/0) in WGS84, generalized with a 0.00002° tolerance and rounded to six decimal places. `config/` also owns shared categories, policy and calculation defaults. `design/` owns visual tokens. Vitest and Playwright cover calculations, APIs, and browser journeys. npm, Wrangler, and maintained Make targets handle application operations.
- Start with `.env.example`, `.env.local.example`, and `.env.prod.example`. Setup creates missing files only and generates the local auth secret. The tooling loads root `.env`, then `.env.local` or `.env.prod` for the explicitly selected environment. Vite’s dotenv loading is disabled, and no private bindings are serialized into the frontend. Opt-in production CI injection can override only `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`, `CLOUDFLARE_TAG_API_TOKEN`, `AUTH_SECRET`, and `OPENAI_API_KEY` when `BLOCKFIT_CI_INJECT=true`. Wrangler receives resolved bindings and provider credentials. Production uses its secret storage.
- Recovery uses **Forgot password? → Contact us** with the configured `CONTACT_EMAIL`. No verification or reset email is sent. Operators independently verify ownership before resetting, since an unverified email claim does not prove identity. Use `make staff-create`, `make password-reset`, or `make session-revoke` with an explicit `ENV`. Passwords are entered at a hidden prompt. Reset revokes existing sessions.

### Demo updates and named accounts

For an existing store, stop the local dev server, run `make db-migrate ENV=local` if migrations are pending, then preview the expansion with `make seed-update ENV=local`. Apply with `make seed-update ENV=local APPLY=yes`, then run `make seed-goals ENV=local` for the synthetic goal scenarios. The expansion preserves existing rows and fills in new fixture IDs. An upgrade from the original 0.0.1 fixture still replaces that older synthetic demand sample. Fresh setup installs the expanded dataset automatically.

Copy [the account definition example](config/accounts.example.json) into `.blockfit/local-accounts.json` or `.blockfit/prod-accounts.json` and fill in the operator-approved logins, display names, roles, and purposes. Use `make accounts-provision ENV=local` to preview changes, then repeat with `APPLY=yes`. Use the explicit `ENV=prod` target only when authorized. Named presentation passwords are entered in a hidden terminal prompt. One dedicated automated-test resident and one staff account per target can use the protected smoke credentials described in the [operations guide](doc/operations.md).

Ordinary resident registration remains available. Staff provisioning requires operator access. Existing role or purpose conflicts require explicit resolution. Automated-test requests use their own comparison and revision scope, including requests created through proposal review. All staff can manage equipment types and goals, review proposals, and evaluate goals in their authorized comparison scope. Shared inventory mutations and the existing overall Explain action require ordinary staff access.

## Cloudflare operations

The public demo is served at the Pages project’s assigned `pages.dev` URL. Set `PAGES_PROJECT_NAME` in `.env.prod` (the example requests `blockfit-demo`). Provisioning reads the assigned hostname and uses it as the canonical origin for Pages, the Worker, and Better Auth. An optional `APP_ORIGIN` pins the exact value. The Worker and D1 use `blockfit-nyc-prod[-suffix]` by default. Pages invokes the Worker through a private service binding. The Worker’s `workers.dev` route and version preview URLs are disabled. Pages preview and deployment aliases cannot enter the app. See the [deployment architecture](doc/architecture.md).

Set the Cloudflare account ID, recovery email, and auth secret in the ignored production environment. Wrangler’s existing OAuth login can manage Pages, Workers, and D1. Use an account-owned `CLOUDFLARE_TAG_API_TOKEN` with Resource Tagging read/write permissions when using OAuth. Alternatively, a scoped `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` can handle both deployment and tags. Store tokens privately. The protected manifest in `.blockfit/` records the account, Pages project, Worker, and D1 database. For a parallel deployment, select a unique Pages project name and deployment suffix.

Run `make provision ENV=prod DRY_RUN=yes` to check the target without creating cloud resources. After authorized provisioning and deployment, run the production smoke test and check actual Worker CPU before broader testing. [Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/) · [D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/)

| Command | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `make provision ENV=prod` | Reconcile the Pages project and D1 database, tag both, and apply migrations. Use `DRY_RUN=yes` for read-only target verification. |
| `make deploy ENV=prod` | Deploy the private Worker, verify its public routes are off, then deploy the Pages ingress. Use `DRY_RUN=yes` for target verification and packaging. |

| `make suspend ENV=prod` / `make resume ENV=prod` | Close or reopen the site and API while retaining D1. |
| `make snapshot ENV=prod SNAPSHOT=<private path>` | After suspension and draining in-flight writes, export portable data and compatibility metadata. |
| `make restore ENV=prod SNAPSHOT=<private path>` | Import into a separately named, fresh D1 target. Validate records before resume. |
| `make destroy ENV=prod` | Confirm the exact account, Pages project, Worker, D1 UUID, and data retention choice. Independent backups stay outside the target. |

The workflow uses the Cloudflare Resource Tagging API for Pages, Worker, and D1 metadata. Values come from the resolved configuration. Defaults are `project-name=blockfit-nyc`, `environment=prod`, `managed-by=script`, `service-part=infra`, and `funding=personal`. Tags help inventory resources. Track usage for the named Worker and D1 database alongside billing and shared account limits. Native Worker logging records compact errors with correlation IDs. Routine invocation logs and tracing are disabled. [Resource tagging](https://developers.cloudflare.com/resource-tagging/) · [Logging pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/observability/pricing/)

Local auth uses Better Auth's native scrypt parameters. Median native scrypt verification was about 37 ms of Node process CPU on the development machine. Median staff sign-in took about 39 ms wall time through local Workerd and D1. The Cloudflare Free Worker allowance is 10 ms CPU per request, so validate actual Workers CPU for sign-up, login and password change during authorized production testing. Deployment and live Cloudflare verification are pending. [Workers CPU limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/#cpu-time)

## Optional AI and portable data

City staff can request a cached OpenAI explanation of the deterministic comparison. The input contains aggregate counts, site labels, cost ranges, budget fit, and the code-selected highlight. Individual locations, identities, and request text stay in BlockFit. The app calculates coverage, costs, and rankings before AI runs.

For a selected goal, **Evaluate goal** assesses options in batches as **Strong**, **Partial**, **Weak**, or **Insufficient evidence**. Choose **Use equipment and dates only** to save requirements without a qualitative objective. Add an objective to run a goal evaluation. The staff comparison uses the ordinary table and explanation until **Evaluate goal** returns option assessments. The goal-fit column, filters, and ordering controls then become available. Fit filters apply the separate budget, feasibility, and structured goal checks. Excluded and unknown options remain available in the full view. Notes, schedules, costs, and observed request counts can inform the assessment. The app has no verified demographic, accessibility certification, or delivery data. **Request AI explanation** uses the current goal, assessment, filters, and order. With assessments, it opens with the evidence for the first visible option. Changing the applied goal, filter, order, or comparison clears the explanation. Request it again for the new view. **Download brief** includes that view and baseline figures without generating AI. Clear the goal to restore the ordinary view.

Explain uses separate Worker-only prompts for [ordinary comparisons](prompts/investment-comparison/explain-investment-options.txt) and [comparisons with a program goal](prompts/investment-comparison/explain-goal-investment-options.txt). Named placeholders such as `{{output_word_limit}}` are filled from validated configuration. Run `make test` for mocked provider checks and `make package ENV=local` to verify the Worker bundle. Effective instructions, output schema, model, and comparison data participate in cache invalidation.

The three AI features use the root `.env` model, `gpt-6-luna`, with `OPENAI_REASONING_EFFORT=low` and the Standard service tier. Requests use capped input and generated tokens with `store:false`. Staff evaluation and explanation share a five-second interval per account between uncached requests. Cached results remain available during the interval. Provider retention rules still apply. AI defaults to disabled in deployment configuration. Comparison and export work independently. [Goal assessment prompt](prompts/investment-goals/evaluate-program-goal.txt) · [Goal limits](config/goals.ts)

Set each environment's project API key and `AI_ENABLED=true` to enable generation. Manage spend and model access in the OpenAI project. BlockFit checks the provider’s input count before starting generation. Goal evaluations split into batches with output sized per option. Explain sends compact metrics for every option and detailed assessment notes for leading visible options. If the comparison exceeds the configured AI request size, the app estimates a smaller selection from the measured usage and asks staff to select fewer sites. D1 enforces the cooldown and prevents duplicate work. [Staff comparison limits](config/settings.ts) · [OpenAI model](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna) · [Project limits](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9186755-managing-projects-in-the-api-platform) · [Data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)

The resident **Explore travel options** panel and **Explore this location** action use the same AI gateway as staff explanations. Set a reference pin or explicitly use your saved request pin and choose equipment. Sign in as a resident and select **Generate suggestions** for a shortlist, or **Generate suggestion** for a specific site. Choose **Show on map** on a shortlisted option to close the chooser, select the equipment, and find its suggestion, Maps links, and copy action below the map. An anonymous sign-in handoff keeps the origin in tab storage until it is consumed. Suggestions share a five-second cooldown per resident. The account-scoped cache lasts up to 24 hours, subject to the shared cache cap. Cached results are available during cooldown. [Travel configuration](config/travel.ts) · [Resident prompts](prompts/resident-travel)

OpenFreeMap Liberty uses the `openmaptiles` source. The inspected Forest Hills z14 tile exposed `poi` Point features with `class=bus, subclass=bus_stop` and `class=railway, subclass=subway/station`. The adapter checks those fields, uses `name_en`, `name:en`, or `name`, and deduplicates by vector feature ID or rounded coordinates, mode, and name. The current TileJSON publishes POIs at z11–14. Collection starts at map zoom 14 or above and inspects only loaded tiles, capped at 1,024 features, with an eight-second wait and an 800 m search around each endpoint. All observations are treated as partial. The shortlist keeps distance order, and observations can only support neutral context about mapped landmarks at both ends. Missing evidence falls back to equipment facts, straight-line comparisons, and external Maps links. Existing OpenFreeMap attribution stays visible.
